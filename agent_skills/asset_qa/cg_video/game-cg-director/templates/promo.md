# Promo template

Show the game category, its distinctive playable promise, and visible proof of that promise.

- Establish genre or playable situation, then expose the hook early through a character, verb, tool, vehicle, mechanic, or mission problem.
- Prove one feature per beat: setup → character action → system, opponent, or world response → payoff. Do not substitute title cards or claims.
- Deepen the same promise with a harder use, new interaction, tactical variation, or larger consequence before adding another feature.
- When coordinated actors recur, keep their visible count, roles, formation, movement paths, targets, and objective ownership stable only to the detail needed for the demonstrated feature.
- Preserve actionable geography when understanding the feature depends on actor positions, entrances, boundaries, cover, targets, routes, or objectives. Establish the relevant relationship before an irreversible action and keep paths or lines of action non-conflicting.
- Choose camera side, height, and movement to support the requested player fantasy while keeping actor-to-target direction readable; a dramatic angle must not reverse the action or hide its result.
- End with evidence of play or scope: an objective completed, system transformed, challenge escalated, or requested reveal.
- Preserve supplied title or interface text; invent no slogans, logos, UI, awards, or claims. Attach sound to actions; let music shape progression, not replace it.
