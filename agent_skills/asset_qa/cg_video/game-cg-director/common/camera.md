# Camera vocabulary

Write camera movement as a natural action inside the shot. Use a motion type plus amplitude and speed only when range or pace matters.

| Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Zoom In / Zoom Out | Change focal length while the camera stays in place. |
| Push In / Pull Out | Move the camera forward or backward. |
| Pan Left / Pan Right | Pivot horizontally from a fixed position. |
| Truck Left / Truck Right | Translate the camera horizontally. |
| Tilt Up / Tilt Down | Pivot vertically from a fixed position. |
| Pedestal Up / Pedestal Down | Move the camera vertically. |
| Arc Shot | Move around the subject on an arc. |
| Tracking Shot | Follow a moving subject. |
| Static Shot | Keep position and lens still. |
| Shake Slightly / Shake Strongly | Apply slight or strong shake. |
| POV | Show the subject's point of view. |
| Roll Clockwise / Roll Counterclockwise | Rotate around the lens axis. |

Use `with small amplitude`, `with large amplitude`, `at slow speed`, or `at fast speed` only when meaningful. Do not stack unexplained camera terms at the end.

A focus change is not camera movement. When requested, state the source and destination of focus, whether framing stays static, and what is sharp at the end. Do not add camera travel during a focus change unless the user requests both.

## Stage the camera

- Give each shot a visual task: establish space, reveal a subject, follow an action, show a reaction, or hold the result. Camera movement must serve that task rather than decorate it.
- When two sides, a boundary, or a directional action can be confused, state the camera's position relative to them, the lens direction, the subject's facing or travel direction, and the target. Prefer positive geometry over negative warnings.
- Preserve the established line of action and screen-side relationship across cuts. Cross it only when the transition visibly re-establishes the new geography.
- Frame the complete motion path when an action depends on body mechanics, a prop, or a target. Use a closer view only after their relationship is established or remains visible.
- For a follow or tracking move, name the followed subject, the camera's relative position, and the end framing. Do not let a background subject become the implied camera target.
- End each moving shot in a composition that either shows the requested result or hands a clear spatial anchor to the next shot.

Frame the action source, direction, target, and result readably. Use one dominant camera move per short shot, compatible with subject motion; reduce it at decisive contact or reveal. Cut instead of forcing one move to reverse sides, pass through a boundary, or perform several unrelated visual tasks.
